Spending over time

A decade of growth — almost all of it public safety

One argument in the property tax debate is that rising home values have produced surplus revenue and that spending has grown faster than the cost of services. Adjusted for inflation and population, the numbers tell a different story: real spending per resident grew modestly, 83% of the real growth went to public safety, and spending on everything else fell per resident. If Amendment 3 forces cuts, they land on a budget whose growth has overwhelmingly gone to the Sheriff's Office and Fire & Rescue.

Nominal and inflation-adjusted figures differ

From 2015 to 2024, total governmental-activities spending rose 87.5% in raw (nominal) dollars. Over the same period the dollar lost value and Jacksonville added residents. Adjusting for inflation, real spending grew 42.2%. Adjusting further for the 16.6% population growth, real spending per resident grew about 22% over nine years, or roughly 2.2% a year.

Where did the real increase go?

Of the real spending growth between 2015 and 2024, about 83% went to public safety: the Sheriff's Office, Fire and Rescue, and the Medical Examiner. The remaining real growth was spread across the other categories of city spending. The chart below isolates how non-public-safety spending changed over the same period.

How Jacksonville compares to its peers

Jacksonville is not a high-spending city catching its breath — it is the leanest large government we compared. In our FY2025 peer study of 14 city and consolidated governments, Jacksonville spends the least per resident of any of them, while devoting a larger share of that smaller budget to public safety than the typical peer. There is no cushion of discretionary spending to absorb a $283 million annual cut.
