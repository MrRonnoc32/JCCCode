What is locked, and what is exposed

Public safety, debt service, and pension obligations are permitted uses of property tax — hard to cut. The gap has to be absorbed by everything else: the exposed pool.

The $283.2M gap is about 41% of the $692.5M exposed pool — but only about 14% of the ~$2.00B total. The squeeze falls on the flexible third of the budget, not the whole thing.

How the revenue figures work. The property-tax backfill is the primary estimate: it raises the rate on the reduced taxable base that remains after the exemption (about $81.1M per mill, versus $106.4M today), so it runs higher than a simple scale-up. The sales-tax-equivalent uses about $235M per one-cent Duval surtax (EDR FY2026 estimate; illustrative). Both are scale comparisons for understanding, not automatically available policy options — each revenue source has its own legal caps, uses, and approval rules.
