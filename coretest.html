The core-services test

This is the amendment's other change. Beyond cutting revenue, it would restrict property-tax dollars to a fixed list of "core" uses. This tool sorts the City's actual budget against that list to ask: how much of what Jacksonville spends today is even an allowable use of property tax?

What the amendment allows property tax to fund

Under the amendment, local property-tax revenue may only pay for these core uses:

  • Public safety — law enforcement, fire rescue, and EMS
  • Education and public schools
  • Infrastructure — roads, bridges, and stormwater
  • Natural resources and flood control
  • Local bonds and existing debt service
  • Employee retirement obligations and pensions
  • Operations and administration of county and city government — and expenditures approved by their governing bodies

That last item is the question. How strictly is it read?

How to read this. Every line of the Council Auditor's FY2025-26 budget is sorted into one of the seven allowable uses or marked "outside the core." Public safety, debt, pensions, and infrastructure are clear; libraries (counted as education), parks and recreation, economic incentives, health and human-services spending, and grants are judgment calls, flagged below. This models only the spending restriction — it is separate from the $283M revenue cut, and the City has other (capped) revenue that could in theory carry some non-core spending.

About this tool. The classification is the Office of Research's reading of the bill, not a legal determination, and the "operations and administration / approved by the governing body" clause has not been tested in court. Reasonable people will sort some lines differently — that is exactly why the narrow/broad toggle exists. State-mandated payments the City cannot choose to drop — the Medicaid county contribution (F.S. 409.915) and the juvenile-justice county share — are treated as required obligations rather than discretionary spending.
