Services at stake

The cuts in the Consequences tool would land here. These are the everyday services funded by the City's non-protected budget — what residents actually receive, by department, alongside each department's General Fund cost. This is what a deep, sustained cut would put at risk.

Public Library

$39.0M / yr
  • 2.2 million visits and 137,000 active cardholder households — over 32% of Duval County (2024).
  • 7.5 million items checked out (5.9M physical, 1.6M digital) in 2024.
  • 139,000 program attendances, in person and virtual (2024).
  • 846,000 public computer and wifi sessions (2024).

Source: Jacksonville Public Library FY24 Annual Report.

Public Works

$63.5M / yr
  • Maintains 3,400+ miles of paved roads, about 4 miles of unpaved roads, and 1,000+ miles of drainage ditches. (Stormwater maintenance is funded separately by the stormwater fee.)
  • Maintains and secures roughly 800 public buildings totaling 9 million square feet.
  • 113 miles of road improved and 186,000+ acres mowed in the past 12 months.
  • Tree planting: 1,227 trees in 2026 (goal 2,395); 20,499 planted since 2018.
  • MyJax (630-CITY): 109,288 service requests in 2025, 76.8% completed on time.

Sources: City of Jacksonville Public Works, tree-planting, and MyJax dashboards.

Parks, Recreation & Community Services

$57.2M / yr
  • Runs the parks system, community centers, pools, and youth and senior programs.
  • MyJax (630-CITY): 9,776 parks service requests in 2025, 85.6% completed on time.

Source: City of Jacksonville MyJax dashboard.

Neighborhoods

$12.2M / yr
  • MyJax (630-CITY): 164,618 service requests in 2025 — the largest volume of any department — 92.5% completed on time.
  • Animal Care & Protective Services: 30,000+ calls a year, caring for 6,000–8,000 animals; 8,599 intakes and 19,816 volunteer hours in the last 12 months; 78.2% save rate. (The City lists Animal Care under Administrative Services.)
  • Housing & Community Development (CAPER PY24) — unverified, source not confirmed: roughly 3,102 people served by public-facility improvements; 8,548 through special-needs public services; 5,972 through employment training, childcare, youth services, and food banks; plus homeless (ESG) and HOPWA housing assistance.

Sources: MyJax and Animal Care dashboards. HCD figures are unverified (no confirmed source).

Office of Administrative Services

$16.0M / yr
  • Solid Waste: 41.4 million curbside pickups in the last 12 months, 99.94% completed successfully. (Collection is funded mainly by the solid-waste fee; the department also covers procurement, fleet, and internal services.)

Source: City of Jacksonville Solid Waste dashboard.

Planning & Development

$4.6M / yr
  • Building inspection issues about 9,000 permits a month.
  • JAXEPICS permitting (2025): 97,500 permits issued of 101,700 submitted; 79,000 issued on first submission; about 10 days on average to issue a permit.

Sources: City of Jacksonville Building Inspection and permitting dashboards.

Capital projects in the pipeline

Separately from the operating budget, the City is carrying 296 capital improvement projects worth about $3.05 billion — the roads, bridges, drainage, parks, and facilities work that Public Works staff plan and manage. Capital dollars are budgeted apart from the $283M operating loss, but the people who deliver these projects are paid from the operating budget that the amendment would squeeze.

About these figures. Department budgets are the Council Auditor's FY2025-2026 General Fund / GSD amounts; usage metrics are the most recent figures published on City dashboards and annual reports, linked above. Where a figure could not be tied to a confirmed source it is labeled unverified. Usage years vary by source (2024–2026) and some City dashboards reset their counts mid-year.
